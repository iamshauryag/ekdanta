Why Slow Writing Matters

I used to think slow writing was a kind of vanity, a way of dressing up hesitation as craft. Then I spent a winter drafting one essay a week, and the weeks that produced the worst pages were always the ones I rushed.

Speed gives you the first idea that arrives, and the first idea is rarely yours. It is the common one, worn smooth by everyone who reached for it before you. Slowness is how you get past it, to the second and third, where the strange and particular things live.⌘1 · RECALL2 of your sources “Patience is not a mood. There is a physical part to it as well. When I write by hand the sentence has to survive the whole length of my arm before it reaches the page, and most of the lazy ones do not make the journey. On a keyboard they arrive intact and I leave them there out of politeness.

What I have stopped doing is apologising for the pace. A slow paragraph is not a failed fast one; it is a different kind of object, one that has been looked at from more than one side. I would rather hand a reader four sentences I trust than forty I merely finished.

Why Slow Writing Matters

I used to think slow writing was a kind of vanity, a way of dressing up hesitation as craft. Then I spent a winter drafting one essay a week, and the weeks that produced the worst pages were always the ones I rushed.

Speed gives you the first idea that arrives, and the first idea is rarely yours. It is the common one, worn smooth by everyone who reached for it before you. Slowness is how you get past it, to the second and third, where the strange and particular things live.

There is a physical part to it as well. When I write by hand the sentence has to survive the whole length of my arm before it reaches the page, and most of the lazy ones do not make the journey. On a keyboard they arrive intact and I leave them there out of politeness.

What I have stopped doing is apologising for the pace. A slow paragraph is not a failed fast one; it is a different kind of object, one that has been looked at from more than one side. I would rather hand a reader four sentences I trust than a hundred that only happened to arrive.
