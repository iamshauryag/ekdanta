On Writing Slowly

Essaydraft287 words

I used to judge a writing day by the number at the bottom of the page. Two thousand words meant I had earned dinner; four hundred meant I had failed in some way I couldn't name. Years later, the days I was proudest of were rarely the ones I still liked.

Slow is not the same as stuck. Stuck is staring at the cursor and resenting it. Slow is staying with one sentence long enough to hear what it is trying to say, and then letting it say less. The sentence usually gets shorter. The thought, oddly, gets larger.

My grandmother wrote letters this way, though she would never have called it a method. She wrote on Sunday afternoons at the same table, and she crossed things out rather than starting over. Reading them now, I can watch her change her mind, which is what I trust most about them.

That is what I want from my own pages now: not polish, but evidence that someone was paying attention. A reader can feel the difference between a sentence that was chosen and a sentence that merely arrived.
