Why Slow Writing Matters

I used to think slow writing was a kind of vanity, a way of dressing up hesitation as craft. Then I spent a winter drafting one essay a week, and the weeks that produced the worst pages were always the ones I rushed.

⌘5 · Passagemake this less abstract↵ refine · Esc

Rushing hands you the first idea that arrives, and the first idea is rarely yours. It is the common one, worn smooth by everyone a stair worn hollow by everyone who reached for it before you. Slowness is how you get past it, to the second and third, where the strange and particular things the odd, specific details a reader remembers live.

Original

There is a physical part to it as well. When I write by hand the sentence has to survive the whole length of my arm before it reaches the page, and most of the lazy ones do not make the journey. On a keyboard they arrive intact and I leave them there out of politeness.

Suggestion

Handwriting adds a bodily test. Each sentence must travel down my arm to reach the paper, and the careless ones give up on the way. On a keyboard they slip through untouched, and I leave them standing, to be polite.
